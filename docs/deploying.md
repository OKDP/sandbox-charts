# Deploying these services

A platform deploys the services of this catalog as instances of
[okdp-compiler](https://github.com/OKDP/okdp-compiler)'s tree:

```text
platform.yaml                                  the platform's values and connections
projects/<project>/project.yaml                a project, the namespace of its instances
projects/<project>/instances/<instance>/
  instance.yaml                                {service, version}: a package, catalog/<service>/<version>/
  config.yaml                                  its values (the package's config.schema.json)
```

The release of an instance is `<project>-<instance>`. Nothing orders the instances but
the deployment graph, the connections they reference and the claims they bind: an
instance applied before the CRDs or the webhook it needs is retried by the engine
until they exist.

The sandbox, [`OKDP/okdp-sandbox`](https://github.com/OKDP/okdp-sandbox), is such a
platform. Its tree is its `gitops/` directory, where each instance's `config.yaml`
says what the instance is for. This page lists them and does not copy their values.

## What the sandbox deploys from this catalog

| Instance (`<project>/<instance>`) | Service | Version | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `okdp-system/local-secrets-provider`, `demo/secrets` | `local-secrets-provider` | `1.0.0-1.0.0` | External Secrets (the credentials it generates), the platform's secret store (those it reads, `remoteKey`, keys of the instance's namespace) |
| `cnpg-system/cnpg-postgresql` | `cnpg-postgresql` | `18.3.0-1.0.0` | the CloudNativePG operator, External Secrets and the platform's secret store |

## What the platform needs before anything compiled

The platform's dependencies are not packages of this catalog. OKDP compiles none of
them: they must run before any compiled instance can. The sandbox installs them with
helm, from this repository's [`bootstrap/`](../bootstrap) (its
[README](../bootstrap/README.md), `bootstrap/charts.yaml`), which okdp-sandbox's
`scripts/bootstrap.sh` installs:

- cert-manager, trust-manager, the cluster issuer `default-issuer` and the CA bundle
  of every namespace (`certs-bundle`);
- Reloader and the admission policies (ValidatingAdmissionPolicies): the deletion
  protection (`okdp.io/protected`) and `okdp-pod-bounds`, the bounds of the pods of
  a ServiceAccount a ConfigMap labelled `okdp.io/pod-bounds=true` names (jupyterhub's
  PySpark sessions);
- ingress-nginx, the cluster's CoreDNS patched to answer the ingress suffix, the host's
  DNS server;
- the CloudNativePG operator;
- External Secrets;
- Keycloak, its database a CloudNativePG `Cluster`;
- the S3 store (RustFS);
- the platform's secret store (OpenBao), which the SecretStore of each project's
  namespace signs in to (`global.okdp.secrets`);
- Forgejo and its CI runner;
- Flux or Argo CD.

`platform.yaml` declares what the services need of them: the ingress class and the
cluster issuer as `global.okdp.ingress` and `global.okdp.tls`, Keycloak as
`global.okdp.oidc`, the secret store as `global.okdp.secrets`, the S3 store as the
class `s3` of the claims (`global.okdp.provisioning.s3`, the provider
`s3-system/s3-minio-provisioner`: a bucket and an identity limited to it per
claim), and, for the demo, whose services share the platform's buckets, as a
connection of its project (`platform-s3`, contract `s3`, its credentials in the
secret store under the demo's keys, `secretRef: {remoteKey: demo/s3/platform}`,
which the bootstrap writes for the namespaces of `bootstrap/openbao/s3.yaml` only).

## Optional services

No service of this catalog is a hard requirement of the platform. Each can be replaced
by a cluster's own service, or by an external one:

- `s3-minio-provisioner`: the provider of the claims of buckets on the S3 store the
  platform installs itself (the sandbox's RustFS, or a company's MinIO), through
  MinIO's admin API. Without it, the services name the platform's `s3` connection and
  share its identity. A platform that deploys it gives it a project of its own
  (`s3-system`, say). That project declares the store as a connection whose
  credentials are a provisioning identity (`secretRef: {remoteKey:
  s3-system/s3-provisioner}`), which the sandbox's bootstrap creates in RustFS and
  writes to OpenBao. Its instance names that connection (`store`), and a class of
  `platform.yaml` names the instance
  (`catalog/s3-minio-provisioner/1.0.0-1.0.0/README.md`).
- `seaweedfs`: an external S3 store serves as well, a connection of `platform.yaml` (the
  sandbox's RustFS, installed with helm). A platform that deploys it as an instance
  (`default/storage`, say) starts from
  `catalog/seaweedfs/4.47.0-1.0.0/ci/sandbox-values.yaml`, and provides the Secret of its
  grants, `creds-seaweedfs-s3` (`accessKey`, `secretKey`), generated by
  `local-secrets-provider` for instance. It provisions the claims of `s3` connections
  when a class names it.

## Platform values these services read

`platform.yaml`, under `global.okdp`:

- `ingress.suffix`, `ingress.className`;
- `tls.clusterIssuer`: the sandbox's is `default-issuer`, of its bootstrap;
- `tls.caBundleSecret`: the sandbox's is `certs-bundle`, the Secret that the Bundle of
  its bootstrap writes in every namespace;
- `storageClass.data` (`cnpg-postgresql`), `storageClass.workspace` (`seaweedfs`);
- `oidc`: identity is Keycloak's, the sandbox's realm `okdp`, with
  `oidc.clientProvisioning` either `existing` or `dcr` (registration with an initial
  access token, `oidc.dcr.authMethod: initialAccessToken`; the realm refuses anonymous
  registration);
- `provisioning.<contract>` and `secrets.store`, read by the claims (below);
  `secrets.store`, `secrets.provider` and `secrets.refreshInterval`, read by
  `local-secrets-provider` for the Secrets it reads from the platform's secret store
  (`remoteKey`).

## Connections between the services

A provider publishes its connections (`provides`), each named, in the `okdp.info.yaml`
beside its compiled umbrella chart. The consumers in other namespaces **claim** their
connection, and the provider of its class provisions it. The platform's classes name
the providers, one class per contract, and the default class:

```yaml
# platform.yaml
global:
  okdp:
    provisioning:
      database-server:
        default: standard
        classes:
          standard: {provider: cnpg-system/cnpg-postgresql}   # <namespace>/<instance>
      s3:                       # the provider of the buckets, s3-minio-provisioner on the
        default: standard       # platform's own store, or seaweedfs deployed as an instance
        classes:
          standard: {provider: s3-system/s3-minio-provisioner}   # or default/storage
    secrets:
      store: okdp-secrets       # the SecretStore the compiler writes in each project's
      provider: {vault: ...}    # namespace from provider (the sandbox's OpenBao), each
                                # namespace its own identity; none at bootstrap
```

- The projects' services → `cnpg-postgresql`: the sandbox's `demo/airflow`
  (`OKDP/platform-charts`) leaves its `db` unset, a claim of the default class (its
  package's default). `cnpg-system/cnpg-postgresql` creates the database
  `demo_airflow_db`, its owner role and the role's credentials. It publishes the
  connection under the claim's ID, `demo.airflow.db`. The credentials cross from
  `cnpg-system` to `demo` through the secret store: a `PushSecret` of the provider and
  an `ExternalSecret` of the claimer, both written by the compiler. The release
  `demo-airflow` waits for the provider's. Nothing is declared by hand. (The
  sandbox's Keycloak, installed with helm, has a CloudNativePG `Cluster` of its own.)
- The projects' services → S3. The sandbox's store, installed with helm, is no
  connection of the platform: no namespace reads the platform's identity but the
  demo, whose services share its buckets (one lakehouse), through a connection of
  its project, `platform-s3`, which their `s3` references name, its key
  `demo/s3/platform`. No package claims a bucket by default. A claim that the
  instance writes (`{}`, a class, a provider) binds to the provider of its class:
  `s3-system/s3-minio-provisioner` for the platform's own store, or `default/storage`
  when a platform deploys seaweedfs. The provider creates a bucket and an identity
  limited to it, whose keys cross to the project's namespace through the secret
  store.

A project adds classes of its own (`global.okdp.provisioning` of its `project.yaml`),
connections, and named claims (`claims`): one connection, shared by the instances that
name it.

A claim's binding is computed at each compile, never stored. A changed default class,
or a class's provider, moves its claims to new, empty connections, and the data stays
at the former provider (`okdp-compile --repository` prints each binding). The
providers keep the data of a claim that is gone (the database, the bucket): an
operator removes it.
